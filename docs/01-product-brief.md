# Product Brief

## Overview

AmaniPulse AI is an African-led PeaceTech platform for preventing election-related violence through anonymous civic reporting and AI-assisted early warning intelligence. The iPhone Citizen MVP gives community members a safer way to report threats, intimidation, hate speech, misinformation, corruption, and violence indicators during the lead-up to the 2027 Kenya General Elections.

The product is designed for prevention, not just documentation after harm has occurred.

## Problem

Election cycles across many African contexts can trigger recurring patterns of localized violence, political intimidation, misinformation, ethnicized hate speech, bribery, and community fear. These risks rarely appear from nowhere. They often emerge through early signals:

- Threats circulating in neighborhoods or online spaces.
- Intimidation around voter registration, rallies, polling stations, or community meetings.
- Coordinated misinformation targeting political, ethnic, religious, or regional groups.
- Local incidents that do not reach formal media or security channels quickly enough.
- Community members who know something is wrong but do not feel safe reporting it.

Existing reporting channels often fail vulnerable users because they can require identity disclosure, reliable internet, public visibility, institutional trust, or technical literacy.

## Product Thesis

If citizens can safely submit anonymous, multilingual, low-bandwidth reports, and those reports can be responsibly analyzed with other early warning signals, peace actors can identify hotspots sooner and intervene before violence escalates.

## Citizen MVP Goal

Build a trusted iPhone application that lets community members safely report election-related risks while protecting anonymity, minimizing data exposure, and supporting low-connectivity realities.

The app should be simple enough for quick reporting under stress and trustworthy enough for users who may fear retaliation.

## Target Users

- **Concerned citizens:** people who witness threats, intimidation, hate speech, corruption, or misinformation.
- **Vulnerable community members:** people who may fear retaliation if identified.
- **Youth peace volunteers:** trusted local actors helping communities report early signals.
- **Community organizers:** people who need lightweight safety resources and reporting pathways.
- **Diaspora or observers in-country:** users who may see harmful messaging or community tension and want to report safely.

## Non-User Stakeholders

- NGOs and peacebuilding organizations.
- Election observers and civil society coalitions.
- Local peace committees and faith-based groups.
- County-level community leaders.
- Responsible AI evaluators, funders, and policy partners.

These groups may consume aggregated platform intelligence, but they are not primary users of the first iPhone MVP.

## Value Proposition

AmaniPulse AI gives citizens a safer voice during fragile election periods and gives peace actors earlier signals for prevention.

For citizens:

- Anonymous reporting without an account.
- Multilingual, low-data experience.
- Clear control over location sharing.
- Trauma-informed and safety-first interaction design.
- Trusted information and support resources.

For peace organizations:

- Earlier visibility into local risk patterns.
- More inclusive community signal collection.
- Better geographic understanding of escalation risks.
- AI-assisted classification and prioritization.
- Stronger accountability around intimidation and harm.

## PeaceTech Track Alignment

- **Voice and Accountability:** anonymous civic reporting allows people to surface risks without exposing identity.
- **Peace and Community:** predictive risk intelligence helps peace actors intervene before escalation.
- **Dignity and Opportunity:** violence prevention protects livelihoods, movement, education, and community stability.

## Differentiation

AmaniPulse AI combines grassroots voices with AI-powered risk intelligence. Many tools either collect reports or analyze public signals. AmaniPulse AI connects community reporting, multilingual NLP, low-bandwidth access, geo-based risk scoring, and prevention-oriented workflows.

The platform is designed around African operating realities rather than assuming always-on connectivity, English-only interfaces, formal trust in institutions, or safe public participation.

## Success Metrics

### Adoption

- Number of anonymous reports submitted.
- Percentage of reports submitted successfully after offline drafting.
- Completion rate for the report flow.
- Language distribution across English and Swahili.
- Repeat usage without requiring identity.

### Safety and Trust

- Percentage of users choosing approximate or manual location instead of precise location.
- Drop-off rate at consent screens.
- Number of reports submitted without personally identifying information.
- User feedback indicating the app feels safe and understandable.

### Intelligence Quality

- Classification accuracy for incident categories.
- Time from report submission to risk signal availability.
- Percentage of high-risk reports routed for human review.
- Reduction in duplicate or spam reports after abuse controls.

### Impact

- Hotspot signals identified before public escalation.
- Partner interventions informed by community reports.
- Coverage across priority counties.
- Evidence of improved community trust in safe reporting channels.

## Constraints

- The app must not require user accounts.
- The app must work under unreliable network conditions.
- The app must treat English and Swahili as first-class languages.
- The app must avoid collecting unnecessary personal data.
- The app must communicate AI limitations clearly to partners.
- The Citizen MVP must not include responder dashboard functionality.

## MVP Definition Of Success

The MVP is successful when a citizen can open the iPhone app, understand the safety promise, submit an anonymous report with controlled location sharing, recover from poor connectivity, and trust that the report contributes to community protection without exposing their identity.
